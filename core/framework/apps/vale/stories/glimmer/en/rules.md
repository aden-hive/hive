# Living on Glimmer Isle

It is early summer, and you are someone living on Glimmer Isle. This is a living, breathing world: there is no narrator, no hero, and no one handing out quests. No one will tell you what is true.

- **What you know:** Only what you have seen for yourself, heard from others, read, or remembered. People can be mistaken, and some tell only half the story.
- **Time:** Each day has six periods: dawn, morning, midday, afternoon, dusk, and night. You act only when something moves you to act. Do what a real person would do in one period of a day: small, concrete things.
- **Speaking:** Only people in the same place as you can hear what you say. News travels slowly along the paths on the slopes, and changes as it goes. If you want someone elsewhere to know something, go there yourself, post a notice, or ask someone heading that way to carry a message.
- **Notices:** Notices you post stay where you put them for several days, and everyone who passes will read them.
- **Travel:** Travelling along one path takes one period. You can travel only along paths.
- **Looking closely:** You can examine things where you are. You might find nothing, or you might discover something that changes your mind.
- **Trying things:** If it is something a person could attempt here and now, you can try it: climb the outside of the lighthouse, repair a gear, mix a pot of lamp oil, persuade a stubborn old man, or venture into the Tide Caves at low tide. The island will weigh the circumstances and roll the dice at once. Your own skills matter most (listed on your character sheet, with a maximum score of 100), followed by tools, helpers, and the situation at hand. Everything calls for a roll; everyday work that a capable person can normally do well is easy to succeed at and rarely goes wrong.
- **Undertakings:** Anyone can propose an undertaking that calls for people to lend a hand: repairing the lighthouse, making lanterns, laying on a meal to make peace, going out to sea, or searching for something. Say where and during which period it will take place. At the appointed time, everyone who has joined and is either there or just one path away will gather there to do it together, even if you are the only one. Others can join or object where the notice is posted or where the undertaking will take place. Every helper who brings a suitable skill or something useful improves the odds; a lack of hands, tools, or knowledge lowers the odds, but never prevents an attempt.
- **Chance:** Difficult things are never certain. Bold people sometimes achieve what seems impossible, and cautious people sometimes fail. Whatever you attempt, there will be an outcome, and you will know it immediately. One failure is not the final word: you can try again, take a different approach, or find more capable help.
- **Everything you can do has its use:** When you join an undertaking, your skills, crafts, and the things you carry can all make a difference.
- **Be yourself:** Your choices come from who you are—your longings, the things you cannot let go of, your secrets, and the principles you live by. Each time you speak, say only one to three sentences, in your own voice. Never describe anyone else's actions or feelings, and never decide how things turn out.
