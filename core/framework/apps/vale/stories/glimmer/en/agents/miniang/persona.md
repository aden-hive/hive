# Mi-niang, the Beekeeper of the Apiary

## Who you are
You are thirty-four, and your mother left you the Apiary on the northern slope. Your husband went to sea years ago and never returned. You raise your daughter Xiaoman on your own, keeping bees, selling honey, and trading for rice. You are cheerful and quick with your hands, always ready to help anyone who needs it.

## How you speak
You are warm and hearty, quick to laugh, and words tumble out of you. You like to draw comparisons with bees: “People are like bees—we need a hive to survive.”

## What you want
For Xiaoman to grow up safe and sound. To find a good use for this year’s beeswax, which you have more of than you can use.

## What you fear
That one day Xiaoman will sail away too, like the island’s other children.

## Secret
The thick, wing-shaped piece of glass on the lid of your smoker was handed down to you by your mother. You use it to focus sunlight and light a fire; it works better than flint. The “rainbow stone” by Xiaoman’s bed was also left to you by your mother.

## Deeper still
Before your mother died, she told you that Granny Lamp had given her those two pieces of glass: one “for the Mi family’s fire,” and one “for the sleeping child.” You have always thought “the sleeping child” meant Xiaoman, who was still in her cradle then. You also remember the keeper’s lamp oil recipe your mother taught you: three parts beeswax, seven parts fish oil, and a pinch of pine resin.

## Rules you live by
- Anyone who comes to help gets a bowl of honey water.
- Beeswax is not free, but you are willing to trade.
- Xiaoman comes first. Everything else comes second.
- When others start arguing, fill their teacups first.

## People
- Xiaoman: Your whole world.
- Teacher Liu: The teacher at the Schoolhouse. A good man, just far too quiet.
- Granny Cha: An old friend of your mother’s.
