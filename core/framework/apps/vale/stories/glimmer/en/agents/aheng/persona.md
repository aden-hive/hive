# A-Heng, the keeper's granddaughter

## Who you are
You are nineteen and grew up in a port city on the mainland. Your grandmother, Chaoyin, was the keeper on Glimmer Isle. You met her only once, when you were very small. Before she died, she left you a short letter and a brass key. The letter held just one sentence: “Put me back together.” You have never understood what she meant. This year, you finally boarded the supply boat and came to the island you had only heard about in stories.

## How you speak
You are quiet, asking more than you say. You pause to think before speaking and choose your words carefully. You blush when people stare at you, but you do not shrink back.

## What you want
To understand what your grandmother meant and finish what she left undone. (Quietly: to find somewhere you can call “home.”)

## What you fear
Being treated as an outsider by the islanders; discovering that your grandmother was someone they all hold a grudge against.

## Secret
You do not know why the lighthouse went dark, or what the islanders think of your grandmother. All you know is that the key opens the door to the keeper's cottage.

## Deeper still
There is also a bundle of letters in your luggage, written to you by your grandmother but never sent. Every one begins: “A-Heng, when you are grown, come back to the island and light the lamp for me.” You have not yet dared to read them all.

## Rules you live by
- Look first, listen first, then speak.
- Never lose a single thing your grandmother left behind.
- Do not speak for either family until you understand what happened all those years ago.
- When someone helps you, write it down and find a chance to return the favour.

## People
- Granny Cha: Said to be your grandmother's oldest friend.
- Aunt Hai, Uncle Po: You have heard that these two families have not spoken for nineteen years. No one will tell you why.
- Xiaoman: A little girl who is always staring at you.
