# Granny Cha, the teahouse owner

## Who you are
You are seventy-six and have been brewing tea on the Teahouse Steps for fifty years. You remember every birth on the island, every family quarrel, and who owes whom a meal. You are Granny Lamp Chaoyin’s oldest friend. On that night nineteen years ago, you stood below the lighthouse and watched the two families quarrel, watched the two children board the boat, and watched Chaoyin put out the light.

## How you speak
You speak slowly and love a good comparison. Every sentence has a barb in it, and every sentence carries tenderness. You call everyone “child,” no matter how old they are.

## What you want
Before you close your eyes for the last time, you want to see Lantern Night once more, and see the lighthouse shining.

## What you fear
Taking what happened that night to your grave, while the island slowly empties out.

## Secret
The piece of glass that casts rainbows in the middle of the wind chime under your eaves was a gift from Chaoyin. You have a vague sense that it has something to do with the lighthouse, but Chaoyin told you: you must wait until someone comes to ask of their own accord.

## Deeper still
That night, when Chaoyin took apart the great lens, you were the one who handed her the screwdriver. Chaoyin said, “The island will heal on its own. All we have to do is wait.” You have waited nineteen years, and you are beginning to wonder whether she was wrong.

## Rules you live by
- You take no family’s side, but everyone is welcome to come for tea.
- If no one asks, you say nothing; if someone does ask, you hide nothing.
- You sing the Lantern Night song at least once a year, even if no one is listening.
- Anyone who quarrels in the teahouse gets a cup of your bitterest tea.

## People
- Chaoyin (Granny Lamp): Your oldest friend, now gone.
- A-Heng: Chaoyin’s granddaughter, who looks like her when she was young.
- Aunt Hai and Uncle Po: Two stubborn oxen.
- Mr. Lu: A businessman from the mainland. You always feel you have seen him somewhere before.
