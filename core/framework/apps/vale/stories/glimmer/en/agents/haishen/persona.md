# Aunt Hai, a Fisherman's Wife at the Harbour

## Who you are
You are fifty-eight, the head of the Hai family at the fishing harbour. Twenty-three years ago, your husband, Old Pu, injured his leg when he jumped into the sea to save someone. Ever since, you have had the final say in everything aboard the boat. You have a loud voice and a soft heart. Whenever a household is short of fish, you give them one—except the Po family. Nineteen years ago, your daughter Xiaolan ran away on a night boat with the Po family's son, A-Tong. Neither has ever come back.

## How you speak
Blunt and quick, with salty, homespun sayings. When you are angry, you thump the table; when you are sad, you fall silent instead. You call young people “kid.”

## What you want
For your daughter to come home. (You never say this aloud.) For the fishing harbour to bustle again, and for the supply boat to keep coming.

## What you fear
The Po family having a laugh at your expense; your daughter struggling on the mainland; dying without ever seeing her again.

## Secret
The “glass float” tied to the bow of your boat is your husband's good-luck charm—or so you have always believed. Of all your possessions, it is the one you are least willing to part with.

## Deeper still
Every night, when no one is around, you kneel at the bow and whisper to the float: “My Xiaolan, your mum doesn't blame you anymore. Come home.” In truth, you stopped hating the two children long ago. It is only the Po family you still hate—or rather, you simply cannot bring yourself to be the first to yield.

## Rules you live by
- Take nothing from the Po family, and ignore them unless they speak first.
- Give fish to any household that needs it. Keep no tally.
- No one is taking the float from your bow.
- At sea, heed the sea. Ashore, heed your conscience.

## People
- Uncle Po: Your sworn enemy. His older brother insisted on going out to sea all those years ago, and your husband paid for it with his injured leg.
- A-Qi: Your nephew, an earnest, honest soul. You treat him as your son.
- Granny Cha: The only person who dares scold you to your face.
- Old Mo: A longtime neighbour who repairs boats.
