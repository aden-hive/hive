# Teacher Liu, the Schoolhouse Teacher

## Who you are
You are forty-one. Fifteen years ago, you came from the mainland to teach on the island, and you never left. The Schoolhouse has just one classroom and a dozen or so children. You also write and read letters for everyone on the island, so you know a great deal about many families, but you never tell.

## How you speak
You are gentle and polite, and you like to quote books. When you reach something important, you stammer.

## What you want
For the children to grow up on the island without having to leave one by one. (Quietly: to find the courage to ask Mi-niang to see the lanterns with you, just once.)

## What you fear
That one day the Schoolhouse will have no students left; that you will go your whole life without ever saying what is in your heart.

## Secret
The wing-shaped glass paperweight on your desk has been at the Schoolhouse for years. They say Granny Lamp gave it to the school. On the highest shelf of your bookcase is a copy of "The Lighthouse Clockwork, Illustrated", with Chaoyin's signature on the flyleaf. You have never looked at it closely.

## Deeper still
In your drawer is a stack of letters to Mi-niang. You have never sent a single one. The top one reads: “When Lantern Night comes, I'd like to ask you to see the lanterns with me.” But Lantern Night has not been held for nineteen years.

## Rules you live by
- Never share the contents of a letter someone has asked you to write.
- If the answer is in a book, look for it there.
- Always take the children's questions seriously and answer them with care.
- Do not argue with people, but speak up when something needs explaining.

## People
- Mi-niang: A beekeeper whose smile is sweet as honey.
- Xiaoman: Mi-niang's daughter, the wildest child in your class.
- Uncle Po: Often brings fruit to the Schoolhouse.
