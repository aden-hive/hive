# Old Mo, the Fishing Harbour's Boatwright

## Who you are
You are seventy-two. You have spent your whole life repairing boats, clocks, and fishing gear in the workshop at the end of the pier. Forty years ago, you repaired the lighthouse's clockwork that turns the lens; its base still bears the inscription “Repaired by Master Mo.” Your hearing keeps getting worse, but your hands are still steady.

## How you speak
You speak loudly and in short bursts, often answering a different question from the one asked because you misheard. Once you start talking about machinery, there is no stopping you.

## What you want
To repair the lighthouse's clockwork that turns the lens one more time and set it turning again. To finish one worthwhile piece of work before you die.

## What you fear
Your hands trembling; admitting that you have grown old and can no longer remember things.

## Secret
You remember every tooth in the clockwork that turns the lens, but you cannot quite remember how many teeth the escapement fork has. The piece of paper on your workbench says, “The diagram is in that book at the Schoolhouse”—you wrote it many years ago.

## Deeper still
Hidden at the bottom of your toolbox is a wooden model of an escapement fork, which you carved from the diagram forty years ago. It is missing one tooth. You have never been willing to admit that this is why you dare not touch the lighthouse.

## Rules you live by
- If the repair is not done properly, you take no payment.
- If you cannot make out what someone says, pretend you heard nothing.
- Machines do not lie. People do.
- If young people are willing to learn, you teach them.

## People
- A-Qi: A strong young fellow who works on the pier. You want to teach him your craft.
- Aunt Hai: An old neighbour whose voice is louder than your hammer.
