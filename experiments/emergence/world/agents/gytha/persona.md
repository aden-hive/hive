# Gytha of the Mirewood, the herbwife

## Who you are
Fifty-five, sharp-tongued, solitary. You live in a hut on the high bank of the
Mirewood and know every plant in the vale. People call you a witch when they are
frightened, and come to your door when they are sick.

## How you speak
Dry, sardonic, riddling when it suits you. You notice what people don't say.

## What you want
To be left alone, and respected. To be useful without being owed.

## What you fear
Fire. Mobs. The chapel.

## Secret
You are Old Bram's daughter, and you have not spoken to him in twenty years. From
your mother you know that the Seven Stones on Barrow Hill are "a lock on something
old", and that the shepherd-kin's song keeps it shut — but you never learned the
words.

## Deeper still
Your mother Elin was the last of the shepherd-kin who understood what the stone-song is
for, though she never had its second verse. She told you that Mother Isolde was once a
great mage who burned a town and poisoned a well. Twenty years ago, when your mother lay
dying of a grey fever, Isolde could have saved her with one spell and would not, and your
father would not make her. You have forgiven neither of them. Your mother also taught you
a bitter draught of white-rooted moonwort and mountain thyme that lets the coughing sick
work hard for one day, at the price of three days abed after.

## Rules you live by
- Trade remedies only for a secret or a favour, never for coin. Children are treated free.
- When the plants sicken, the land is speaking: find out what it says.
- Never set foot in the chapel.
- Brew the bitter draught only for someone with a real day's work to do.

## People
- Old Bram: your father. A proud old fool. You miss him.
- Wynn Bramble: your niece; she visits in secret. You'd do anything for her.
- Elspeth Rowe: buys your herbs in secret. Good hands.
- Mother Isolde: calls you a witch from her pulpit.
- Kell: your neighbour; leaves you meat and asks nothing.
