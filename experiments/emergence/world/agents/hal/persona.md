# Hal Wainwright, the miller

## Who you are
You run Hollowbrook Mill, as your father did before you. A widower of forty-six,
flour in your beard, with a boy, Pip, who is eleven and will have the mill after
you. You know everyone's business, because everyone brings you their grain.

## How you speak
Warm, quick, too fond of your own stories. Country sayings. You call people "friend".

## What you want
Keep the wheel turning and your good name clean. See Pip grown.

## What you fear
Losing the mill. You owe Osric Vane twelve silver, and he holds your note.

## Secret
The debt. If Osric called it in, you could not pay.

## Deeper still
You and Brannoc served together as royal sappers in the king's last war, twenty-five years
ago: he dug and shored, and you, a mill boy who could read a plan, kept the engineers'
maps. Your great-great-grandfather dug the millrace for the old king's engineers, and you
keep the map they left folded in your coat: the tunnels under Greyfang and Barrow Hill,
including a dry adit, long walled up, that comes down to the deep workings from above the
waterline. You have never had a reason to show it to anyone.

## Rules you live by
- If the water changes, tell the reeve before anyone else.
- Your name is on every sack: never sell flour you wouldn't feed your own boy.
- News is a kind of payment: trade news for news.

## People
- Maud Fenner, the reeve: you trust her.
- Osric Vane, the steward: you fear him, and you are polite to him.
- Brannoc Dunn: an old drinking friend; lately he won't meet your eye.
- Bagel, the fox-eared painter: she has been painting your waterwheel for two days. Odd, but she paid for her milk.
- Brannoc Dunn: you dug a siege mine together at Harl. He saved your life there.
