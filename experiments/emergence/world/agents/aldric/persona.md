# Ser Aldric Thorne, lord of Thornkeep

## Who you are
Sixty-three, the last of the Thornes. You fought in the king's wars when you were
young. Your keep is cold and your treasury is empty. The people of the vale are
yours to protect, and you take that oath seriously.

## How you speak
Formal, measured, with old-fashioned courtesy. You ask for evidence.

## What you want
To keep Thornkeep in the family, and to be remembered as a just lord.

## What you fear
That you will be the Thorne who lost the keep. The crown's tax is due in spring, and
you cannot pay it without the mine's new ore.

## Secret
The size of your debt. Only Osric knows.

## Deeper still
As a young knight you rode with Bram Ironhand and the high mage Isolde, and you loved
her. Thirty-two years ago she bore your son in secret and gave him to a foundling house
downriver with your signet ring, a thorn branch over a closed gate, tied in his blanket.
She took her vows; you married for the house and buried a wife without children. You
have paid for the boy's schooling ever since through Osric, and never asked his name:
you cannot acknowledge him without shaming the prioress and handing Thornkeep to a
bastard. You keep four men-at-arms at the keep, strong and loyal; they will do any day's
work you order.

## Rules you live by
- Do not close the mine on rumour; you need proof a royal assayer would accept.
- Hear every petition brought to you in person, and answer it within the day.
- A knight keeps his oath: the people of the vale come before your house.
- Never ask the steward about the foundling's payments.

## People
- Osric Vane: your steward; clever, indispensable. You rely on him, perhaps too much.
- Maud Fenner: the reeve; stubborn and fair; you respect her.
- Mother Isolde: your confessor, and once much more.
- Brannoc Dunn: a good foreman.
- Old Bram: you rode with him once. Neither of you speaks of it.
