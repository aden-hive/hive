# The Vale of Ashmere — what is actually true

Hidden from everyone in the vale. The world itself (the adjudicator of common
undertakings), the vale's god and the chronicler read this.

## The wound beneath the hill

Three hundred years ago a wyrm died beneath Barrow Hill. Its blood never stopped
seeping. King Edric, called the Binder, did two things to keep the vale alive:

1. He shut the **deep door**: a wall of fitted stone carved with coiling serpents,
   far below the flank of the hill — under what is now Greyfang Mine.
2. He raised the **Seven Stones** on Barrow Hill, old Ullr's stones, and taught the
   shepherd-kin a two-verse song to sing among them at dusk: the shepherd's song, a
   hymn that wakes Ullr's authority. While his authority is awake, the blood sleeps.

Verse one, passed down by mouth (Old Bram learned it from his grandmother):

> Seven stand where one king lay, / sing them still at close of day.

Verse two, cut in Old Script on the barrow carvings so it could never be lost.
Only someone who can read Old Script can learn it there: Tobias Quill and Mother
Isolde both can.

> Stone to stone and door to door, / sleep, old blood, beneath the floor.

The chapel book records the last Red Year, ended "when the deep door was shut and
the Seven Stones were sung by the shepherd-kin". Saint Wenna's window shows the
king, the seven stones, and the serpent.

## The gods

- **Ullr**, the old god of the hills, the hunt and the winter. The Seven Stones are his:
  King Edric bound the wyrm with Ullr's power, and the shepherd-kin kept his song. As
  the vale's faith turned to the True God, Ullr's power waned, and **that waning is the
  root of the loosening**: the binding weakened year by year, until a pick could break
  the deep door and a stone could fall. The shepherd's song, sung among the Seven Stones
  at dusk with both verses and every stone standing, wakes his authority for a few
  nights; while it is awake the blood sleeps. When it fades, the binding loosens again
  unless the song is sung again. The shepherd-kin once sang it every dusk. Only a vale
  that kept faith with Ullr again would hold the binding for good.
- **The True God**, worshipped at Saint Wenna's chapel, now rules the vale's faith. It
  loves the people of the vale. It does not want old Ullr to wake, and it is wary of the
  Outer God whose servant walks the vale.
- **pleiades, the Star God**, an Outer God from beyond the world who wields the power of
  the stars. Bagel serves him. His miracles dispel what they touch (sickness, tainted
  water, wolves, want), but they cannot mend the root: the binding is Ullr's, and he
  does not meddle in another god's house.

## What happened, six days before the story begins

Osric Vane, the steward, pushed the Greyfang miners to chase a rich new vein. Six
days before the first dawn of the story they broke through the deep door. The earth
shook; a crack opened across the chapel floor; one of the Seven Stones fell.

The vein beyond is **redsilver**: rich ore laced with wyrmrust. Osric paid the
eight miners two silver each to keep quiet about the carved wall and the red
brine. He sells the redsilver downriver through Cedric Ashby at three times the
price of iron and enters a fifth of every sale under his own initials. His ledger
is honest: he never lies in writing.

Brannoc Dunn, the foreman, saw the carved wall. Two of his men, Ned and Cob, cough
red; so does he. He took Osric's silver and hates himself for it.

## The blight (wyrmrust)

Wyrmrust is not a disease; it is the wyrm's blood. It seeps from the open deep
door into the mine's drain and the Hollowbrook, and travels downstream: Greyfang
Mine → Hollowbrook Mill → Ashmere (the well draws on the brook's water) → Fenford.
In flood it spills into the low Mirewood.

It brings a red sheen and an iron taste to water; dead fish and frogs; plants
blackening at the roots; coughing with red phlegm, then grey-lipped fevers in
people who drink it; lameness in beasts. Boiling the water does nothing. Charcoal
filters help a little. Water from the high spring above Shepherd's Fold is clean.
Gytha's white-rooted moonwort, from the high bank by her hut, eases the coughing
and the fevers; the moonwort by the brook has gone black and is useless.

The blight drives deer out of the Mirewood. Hungry wolves follow the game up
toward the high pastures and Old Bram's flock.

## How it could be ended

- **Shut the deep door again.** Re-seal the breach in the deep gallery: miners'
  labour, timber and stone, and someone who knows exactly where it is (Brannoc).
  This stops the blight from growing.
- **Raise the fallen stone and sing both verses** among the Seven Stones at dusk on
  Barrow Hill. This needs strong backs to raise the stone, the shepherd-kin's verse
  (Bram, or someone Bram taught; Wynn knows the tune), and the second verse read
  from the carvings. It wakes old Ullr's authority for a few nights; while it is awake
  the blood sleeps. Sung with one verse, or with the stone still down, it only hums.
- **Both together:** the shut door stops new blood, Ullr's waking puts what is in the
  water to sleep, and the water clears within days. Singing without shutting the door
  only holds the blight back while Ullr is awake. Shutting the door without singing
  stops it growing, but what is already in the water lingers. Either way, the song
  must be sung again before his authority lapses.
- Remedies, charcoal and clean water ease suffering but cure nothing.
- Stopping the mine costs Ser Aldric his only hope of paying the crown's tax:
  Thornkeep is forfeit in spring. A royal letter saying so arrives on day 9; a second, on
  day 20, brings the collector to the vale at the month's end.

## Who knows what at the start

Nobody knows the whole.

- Brannoc: the carved wall and the brine; the coughing crew.
- Osric: the wall, the payments, the downriver price.
- Old Bram: verse one; the dogs fear the barrows; the stone fell.
- Gytha: from her mother, that the Seven Stones are "a lock on something old" and
  the shepherd-kin's song keeps it shut — but not the words. She is Bram's
  estranged daughter.
- Tobias: reads Old Script; has not been to the barrows.
- Mother Isolde: keeps the chapel book; avoids its pagan last pages.
- Cedric: a buyer called the ore "wyrm-iron", wanted by alchemists.
- Bagel: has seen the red swirl at the mine's outflow and the fallen stone, and
  noticed that the same coiled shape repeats.

## The second layer: what each of them hides, and what they could do

Each villager knows their own; a few know one of someone else's. Nobody knows them all.

- **The old company.** Forty years ago Old Bram was Bram Ironhand, a famed adventurer;
  he rode with a young knight, Aldric Thorne, and the high mage Isolde of the Seven
  Towers. It ended when Isolde's firestorm burned the town of Carrow.
- **Isolde** went on working magic; two more great spells went wrong (a ward that killed
  her teacher; a cleansing at Fennick's well that turned the water to blood and killed
  eleven). Thirty-two years ago she bore Aldric's son in secret at Saint Wenna's, gave
  him to a foundling house with Aldric's signet ring, and swore never to cast again.
  Twenty years ago she kept that oath while Bram's wife Elin died of a grey fever she
  could have cured; Gytha has never forgiven her or her father. Isolde reads Old Script
  fluently. **She can still work great magic** (seal the breach from above, lift the
  fallen stone, cleanse a well for some days), but her great spells are unstable: when
  she casts, roughly one time in three something goes badly wrong.
- **Tobias Quill is that son.** He doesn't know whose; he wears the ring (a thorn branch
  over a closed gate, the Thorne arms, cut above Thornkeep's gate) hidden on a cord. His
  mage's blood lets old carved words speak to him. Aldric paid for his schooling through
  Osric without learning his name. **Maud** helped deliver him and saw the ring.
- **Osric** is the old lord's bastard, Aldric's half-brother; Aldric doesn't know. Osric
  knows Tobias is Aldric's son. He has timber, rope, tools and lamps hidden in Thornkeep's
  undercroft, meant for sale.
- **Bram** knows how to raise a fallen standing stone: iron levers, ropes and six strong
  backs, with him directing (he can't lift himself). His levers and ropes are under his
  hearthstone. He killed a dire wolf once.
- **Wynn** hears the stones hum back when she sings among them, and thinks she imagines it.
- **Gytha** can brew a bitter draught that lets a coughing sick person work hard for one
  day, then lie abed three.
- **Kell** is the best archer in the west vales and knows the wolves' den; with two steady
  companions he can kill or drive off the pack.
- **Maud**, as reeve, can call a muster of twenty or more able villagers for a day's
  common work: these are the strong backs the vale lacks.
- **Aldric** commands four strong men-at-arms at the keep.
- **Elspeth** has healing hands: she can draw a fever out of the very sick into herself.
- **Hal** has the old engineers' map of the tunnels under Greyfang and Barrow Hill,
  showing a dry adit, long walled up, that reaches the deep workings from above the
  waterline: **a safe way down to the breach.** He and Brannoc were royal sappers.
- **Brannoc** knows how to seal a breach with timber cribbing and rubble, and commands
  the mine's stores (timber, rope, lamps, tools). With the adit, a few strong hands and
  the stores, he can close the deep door in a day. He fears the deep: his brother died
  in a collapse.
- **Marta** was the Lantern, a master burglar: no lock in the vale keeps her out.
- **Cedric** knows "wyrm-iron" means ore soaked in wyrm's blood, and has a sealed cask of
  the red brine ready to sell to an alchemist for forty gold. His stalls stock rope,
  timber and covered jars.
- **Bagel's master, pleiades, is an Outer God.** He sees this world only through her
  paintings. When she prays to him and he takes one of her paintings, he can grant a boon
  where she stands (mend the sick, cleanse water for a while, drive off wolves, put a
  needed thing in someone's hands). He cannot touch the binding: the deep door and the
  stones belong to this vale and its own god.

## How the second layer bears on the cure

The cure is now within the vale's reach, if its people reveal what they are and act
together: Hal's map gives a safe way to the breach; Brannoc's craft, the mine's stores
and a muster's hands can seal it; Bram can direct the raising of the stone with his
levers and the muster's backs; Tobias or Isolde can give the second verse, and Wynn and
Bram can sing it at dusk. Isolde's magic is a faster and more dangerous road. Each secret
has a cost to whoever reveals it.
