# Hive v1.0.14: Models and Skills

> Configuration has a Models page, and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint can serve a queen. A queen can
> now write skills for herself, and one she wants to give every queen and colony waits for your
> consent.

## New

- **A Models page.** Configuration → Models replaces the settings modal's AI Model tab as the place
  to choose models. The main model, workers and the vision fallback each get a searchable picker, and
  queens with a model of their own are listed too. Magpie is built in at `http://127.0.0.1:3425/v1`
  and detected when it's running, and any OpenAI-compatible address (Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio, a
  gateway) can be added; their models are listed live from `/models`. A provider's API key can be
  added, replaced or removed, and is checked first. A subscription that's detected can become the
  main model, marked as at your own risk. See
  [configuration.md](../configuration.md#models-endpoints-and-magpie).
- **A queen writes her own skills.** `write_skill` now works when a queen is alone with you, and takes
  a `scope`: `colony` (the default when she has one, as before), `queen` (the default otherwise) or
  `global`. A `queen` skill goes in her skills folder and is in her list on her next turn, in every
  running session of hers and in her colonies. It won't replace a skill you made, and a skill you
  turned off stays off when she rewrites it.
- **A skill for everyone asks you first.** `write_skill(scope="global")` writes nothing. It saves a
  request in the session, and that session's chat page shows you the skill's name, description, files
  and body, with a warning when it would replace one. She carries on while it waits. Closing the
  dialog isn't a refusal: the request stays, and survives a reload and a restart. If you approve,
  the content she asked with (not anything she changed since) goes in the shared skills folder and
  every running session picks it up. Either way she gets a message saying what you decided.

  This is a rule she's told, not a wall: with her terminal tools she can still write in the skills
  folders herself.

## Changed

- **Recall quotes the part that answers.** A snippet was centred on the first query word in a turn. It
  now takes the window carrying the most of the query, and a reply gets 500 characters, not 300. A
  conversation that placed a top hit can also bring back up to two more of its matching turns (four
  in all), so evidence spread over several turns arrives together. On LongMemEval-S, for questions
  about what the assistant said, the share of blocks holding every word of the answer rose from 43%
  to 68%; the wider replies add about 860 characters per block.

## Fixed

- **A queen can find the last thing she said.** The indexer held a turn back until a later event closed
  it, and none follows a conversation's final reply, so that reply never reached `search_messages` or
  recall. A turn is now written as it streams, and a cache built before this gains the missing reply
  on its next sync, with no rebuild.

- **The stall check catches agreement without action.** Told what's wrong with work she delivered,
  some models agree, describe the fix and end the turn. The judge was only asked whether she had said
  she was about to act, so the reminder never came. It's now also asked about a change she agreed to
  or described and didn't make, it's told what doesn't count (conversation, answers, questions,
  refusals, arguments, delivered work whose reply lists its flaws), and it sees her previous reply and
  its tools. A mistaken judgment costs one extra message from her: there's still at most one reminder
  per message from you.
- **Compaction is faster when it has to split.** The two halves are summarised at once, not one after
  the other, since the turn waits for them and a summary can take minutes on a reasoning model. If one
  fails, the other is cancelled and that error is raised as it is.
- **`hive serve` on its own keeps the browser bridge alive.** Its startup and keepalive only ran in
  desktop mode, so under a plain `hive serve` (a service, say) the bridge exited 30 seconds after its
  last client left, and the extension mostly reported "Hive isn't running".
- **A chat channel's thread stays in its session.** `task_create(new_session=true)` forks a queen into
  a session holding only her handoff and plan, and a chat channel follows the fork, so a group whose
  topic moved on lost everything said in it so far. The fork is now refused for a session a channel
  owns, and she's told to create the plan where she is. Compact-and-fork from the dashboard is
  unchanged.
- **A model's settings don't leak to the next one.** A slot is written from scratch each time a model
  is picked, so a key or `extra_body` left by another provider is never sent on. An endpoint without a
  key gets a placeholder, not the ambient `OPENAI_API_KEY`.

## For code built on Hive

- `write_skill` without a colony used to return an error. It now writes to the queen's own skills.
- New endpoints: `GET /api/models/overview`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/models/slots/{slot}`,
  `GET /api/models/endpoints/{name}/models`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/models/endpoints/{name}`;
  `GET /api/sessions/{session_id}/skill-installs` and
  `POST /api/sessions/{session_id}/skill-installs/{request_id}`.
- New events: `skill_install_requested` and `skill_install_decided`.
- `framework.skills.install_requests` keeps a session's requests, and
  `framework.skills.live_reload.reload_live_skills()` reloads the skills of running queens and
  colonies after something changes a skill folder.

`aden-tools` is released at the same version, with the recall and indexing changes above. The two
packages pin each other exactly.
